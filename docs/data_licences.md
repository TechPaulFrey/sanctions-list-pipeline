## UK

Open Government Licence v3.0, stated on https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-uk-sanctions-list. The dashboard must show: "Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0."

## UN

The UN terms of use at https://www.un.org/en/about-us/terms-of-use grant permission to download and copy information "for the User's personal, non-commercial use, without any right to resell or redistribute them or to compile or create derivative works therefrom", subject to "more specific restrictions that may apply to specific Material". The page shows no last-updated date.

No terms specific to the Consolidated List were found. The list page https://www.un.org/securitycouncil/content/un-sc-consolidated-list redirects to main.un.org, and its content could not be read by an automated fetch.

Read literally, the general terms conflict with a public dashboard (redistribution), cross-list linking (derivative work) and any reuse in Nexhull (commercial use). The Consolidated List exists so that states and institutions can screen against it, so list-specific terms may be more permissive. This is not verified.

Paul to decide, before the dashboard is published in Phase 7: check the list page in a browser for specific terms, or email the Security Council Subsidiary Organs Branch to ask.

## OFAC

No copyright or reuse statement was found on https://home.treasury.gov/subfooter/site-policies-and-notices or the OFAC FAQ pages checked, and https://ofac.treasury.gov/policies returned 404. OFAC FAQ 90 says members of the public may use the delta file, but does not cover redistribution or commercial use.

Works of the US federal government are generally not subject to copyright in the United States (17 U.S.C. §105), and SDN data is widely redistributed by commercial screening vendors. Neither point is an OFAC statement. Paul to confirm the position before publishing.
